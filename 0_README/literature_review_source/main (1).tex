\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Offshore wind capacity in Europe is projected to grow from approximately 36~GW today to over 300~GW by 2050, with the North Sea and Baltic Sea as the two principal basins. As deployment scales, wholesale electricity prices during high-wind hours are driven down by the merit order effect, eroding the revenue that each turbine earns. This cannibalisation problem is well documented at the national level but has not been systematically compared across the two basins, which differ in wind resource strength, spatial correlation, generation mix, system flexibility, and market design. This literature review synthesises research across three domains that have developed largely in isolation: wind resource modelling, electricity market economics, and GIS-based spatial assessment. It finds that no existing framework integrates all three at the resolution needed to evaluate where offshore wind generates the most revenue, not merely the most energy. Three gaps are identified: the absence of a cross-basin capture value comparison, the exclusion of market value from spatial assessment frameworks, and the lack of a parameterised wind-price model that explains why cannibalisation severity differs across markets. This thesis proposes to address these gaps by developing a model that replaces fixed effects with explicit market parameters, coupling it with hourly wind generation profiles and overlaying the results onto a GIS framework to produce the first revenue-augmented spatial assessment of offshore wind potential across both basins.
\end{abstract}
